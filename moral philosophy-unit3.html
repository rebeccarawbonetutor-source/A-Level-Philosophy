Applied ethics: normative & meta-ethical application
Issue 1: Stealing

Utilitarianism & stealing

Consequentialism

Hedonistic and preference calculations

  • Act hedonism: Weighing immediate physical or psychological pleasure gained by the thief against the acute pain, property loss, and distress suffered by the victim.
  • Preference utilitarianism: Analysing how theft frustrates the long-term rational preferences of owners for security, contrasting this with rare scenarios where stealing food satisfies a vital survival preference.

Kantian ethics & stealing

Deontology

Contradictions and autonomy violations

  • Universal law breakdown: The maxim of theft generates a contradiction in conception; if everyone stole at will, the institution of private property would logically self-destruct, making the act of stealing conceptually impossible.
  • Humanity formulation: Stealing bypasses consent, treating the victim merely as an instrument to satisfy personal desires.

Virtue ethics & stealing

Character

Injustice, greed and practical wisdom

  • Vicious dispositions: Stealing stems directly from the vices of injustice, greed, and deficiency in self-restraint (enkrateia).
  • Flourishing distortion: A person governed by practical wisdom recognises that acts of theft warp the agent's character soul, preventing genuine eudaimonia.

Meta-ethics & stealing

Meta-ethics

Emotivism and prescriptivism

  • Non-cognitive expressions: Declaring that stealing is wrong registers an emotional attitude of revulsion (Emotivism) or issues an imperative command against the act (Prescriptivism).
Issue 2: Simulated killing

Utilitarianism & simulation

Consequentialism

Utility of art versus psychological harm

  • Mill's higher pleasures: Engaging with dramatic tragedies or artistic simulations of war (such as Shakespearean plays) provides profound intellectual and aesthetic utility.
  • Calculus of desensitisation: Conversely, mindless violent video games might increase societal aggression and reduce empathetic utility, creating a net negative outcome.

Kantian ethics & simulation

Deontology

Duties to self and aesthetic boundaries

  • No violation of rational agents: Fictional simulations involve no actual victims or autonomous rights violations, meaning the universal law test does not automatically trigger a contradiction.
  • Self-regarding duties: Kantian ethics cautions that brutal simulations might degrade the moral imagination and weaken internal duties to cultivate sympathetic feelings toward humanity.

Virtue ethics & simulation

Character

Catharsis versus vice habituation

  • Habituation impact: Aristotle examined how tragedy provides emotional catharsis, but excessive immersion in graphic simulated violence risks habituating callous emotional responses, counteracting the development of virtues like compassion and gentleness.

Meta-ethics & simulation

Meta-ethics

Cultural relativism and subjectivism

  • Subjective variance: Ethical subjectivism highlights how judgments on violent media shift across generations and cultures, showing that moral statements about games reflect social tastes rather than objective facts.
Issue 3: Eating animals

Utilitarianism & eating animals

Consequentialism

Sentience calculus and speciesism

  • Singer's critique of speciesism: Failing to count animal suffering equally in the utilitarian calculation is arbitrary discrimination akin to racism or sexism.
  • Net pain calculation: The aggregate suffering inflicted by industrial farming heavily outweighs human dietary pleasure, rendering meat consumption morally impermissible.

Kantian ethics & eating animals

Deontology

Direct status vs indirect duties

  • Exclusion from the moral community: Because animals lack self-consciousness and rational autonomy, they cannot participate in the kingdom of ends, meaning we owe them no direct moral duties.
  • Duty to humanity: Cruelty to animals is nevertheless prohibited indirectly because it coarsens human moral sensibilities, risking cruelty toward fellow humans.

Virtue ethics & eating animals

Character

Temperance, gluttony and empathy

  • Excess and vice: Overconsumption of meat driven by factory farming reflects the vice of gluttony and a lack of proper temperance.
  • Cultivating empathy: A flourishing agent exhibits gentleness and care, questioning industrial cruelty rather than blindly participating in systemic harm.

Meta-ethics & eating animals

Meta-ethics

Intuitionism and moral realism

  • Self-evident wrongs: Moral intuitionism suggests that causing extreme, unnecessary suffering to living creatures is an immediately cognisable, objective moral truth.
Issue 4: Telling lies

Utilitarianism & telling lies

Consequentialism

Act flexibility versus rule stability

  • Act utilitarian allowance: A deceptive white lie told to save someone from deep emotional distress can generate a net surplus of positive utility in specific circumstances.
  • Rule utilitarian prohibition: Hooker's rule utilitarianism insists that maintaining an absolute social rule against lying protects long-term communication channels and maximises general trust.

Kantian ethics & telling lies

Deontology

Strict duty and autonomy destruction

  • Universal law failure: Lying cannot be universalised because it destroys the linguistic preconditions of promising and truth-telling, generating a strict contradiction in conception.
  • Assault on agency: Deception robs the hearer of informed consent, treating their rational capacity merely as a tool for the liar's convenience.

Virtue ethics & telling lies

Character

Truthfulness as a golden mean

  • The virtuous disposition: Truthfulness is an essential character trait located between boastfulness (excess) and mock modesty (deficiency).
  • Phronesis in disclosure: Practical wisdom guides the virtuous agent to balance honesty with tact, knowing when discretion is required without resorting to vicious deceit.

Meta-ethics & telling lies

Meta-ethics

Ethical naturalism

  • Empirical properties: Ethical naturalists argue that wrongness in lying can be reduced to measurable psychological harm, fractured relationships, and the breakdown of social cohesion.
