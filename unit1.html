MoM Unit 1: Substance dualism

Substance Dualism
Nature of mind

Mental states and properties

Foundations

Conscious and unconscious states, intentionality, phenomenal properties (qualia), and introspective access.

Consciousness and the hard problem

* Easy problems: Objective cognitive and behavioral mechanisms.
* Hard problem: Explaining subjective experience itself, creating an explanatory gap (strawberry/brain scanner example).

Metaphysical frameworks and history

Comparison

Contrasting physicalism, substance dualism, property dualism, and historical foundations.

Physicalism, dualism and Plato

* Substance dualism: Minds exist and are distinct from bodies (Mind = thinking/unextended; Body = extended/unthinking).
* Property dualism: Physical substances have irreducibly non-physical mental properties.
* Physicalism: Rejects non-physical substances.
* Plato's dualism: Immortal soul vs mortal body (Argument from Recollection).

Method of doubt and the cogito

* The cogito: Doubting implies thinking; thinking implies existing as a thinking thing.
* Scope warning: Establishes the thinking self, but does not by itself establish substance dualism without further arguments.
Descartes' arguments for substance dualism

Conceivability argument

Deductive

Moving from clear conception to distinct substances.

Formal argument structure

  1. Whatever is clearly conceived is metaphysically possible.
  2. Descartes conceives mind without body, and body without mind.
  3. Separate existence is possible.
  4. Independently existing substances are distinct.
  5. Therefore, mind and body are distinct.

What is conceivable may not be metaphysically possible

Conceivability doesn't guarantee metaphysical possibility due to hidden essential natures (Kripke's water/H2O example).

What is metaphysically possible tells us nothing about the actual world

Possibility does not entail actuality; a disembodied mind *could* exist doesn't prove it *does*.

Mind without body is not conceivable

Supposed disembodied mental acts rely upon bodily concepts.

* Arnauld's criticism: Confusing limits of conception with ontological limits.
* Lycan's masked man analogy: Different descriptions of the same entity.

Indivisibility argument

Leibniz's Law

Distinguishing substances via essential divisibility properties.

Formal argument structure

  1. All physical bodies are divisible by nature.
  2. The mind is indivisible (no spatial parts).
  3. Leibniz's Law: Entities with different essential properties cannot be identical.
  4. Therefore, mind and body are distinct substances.

The mental is divisible in some sense

* Arnauld: Mental faculties act as parts.
* Hume's bundle theory: Introspection reveals shifting perceptions, not a unified substance.
* Split-brain evidence: Independent hemisphere operations (Patient P.S.).

Not everything thought of as physical is divisible

Paul Churchland notes that certain subatomic particles are physical yet lack internal parts or structure.
